How it is organized
The Stanford Natural Language Processing Group describes itself on its own site as "a passionate, inclusive group of students, faculty, postdocs, and research engineers" and states that it "is part of the Stanford AI Lab (SAIL)" with close associations to the Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence and the Center for Research on Foundation Models, and that it includes members of the Linguistics, Computer Science, and Psychology departments and the Graduate School of Education. It is a group inside Stanford University rather than a separate legal entity, so the parent is the university and the group gives its address as the Gates Computer Science Building, 353 Jane Stanford Way, Stanford, California. It is recorded on a system rather than a release: its own page names DSPy, "a Python framework for programming rather than prompting LLMs", and DSPy is the reviewed system record stanfordnlp/dspy, which the group publishes from its own GitHub organization. Nothing in the catalog names the group as a model developer, service operator, runtime maintainer, or steward, so catalog_names is empty and the record joins from the system instead. The group's published software is otherwise Java and Python NLP tooling rather than model weights: CoreNLP, Stanza, and GloVe, which its software page says are "open source, licensed under the GNU General Public License", a different license from the MIT-licensed DSPy, so the record names DSPy as the reviewed system and does not claim the others. It publishes no frontier-safety, responsible-scaling, preparedness, or risk-management framework on its own pages, so the field is omitted rather than filled with the AI Index its affiliated HAI institute produces, which is a research report and not a framework.
